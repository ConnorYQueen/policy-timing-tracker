How to read this dashboard

Start with Summary

Three charts compare the presidents, the senators and the House members. Each president’s tab then shows everything found for that term, ending in a Full Documented Summary.

Colors

Red is Trump (a darker red for his first term) and blue is Biden and Obama (navy for Obama): it marks money that reached the family or connected firms. Gray marks trades placed just before government news, mostly by others; light gray is background, never added up. On the Senate and House pages red is Republican, blue Democrat and dark gray independent; in their trade-value bars the darker part is the lowest possible total and the lighter part runs on to the highest.

How sure is each item?

Documented in official records. Reported by major news outlets, not proven in court. Alleged by lawmakers or watchdogs, not confirmed.

Click anything

Every dot, bar and row opens its record: what happened, who is named and why, what investigators did, and links to every source.

How this tracker works

How sure is each item? Every event carries a label. Documented means it is in official records: court filings, government data, agency releases or financial disclosures. Reported means major news outlets reported it, citing documents or sources; it has not been proven in court. Alleged means lawmakers, watchdogs or people in a dispute claim it; it is not confirmed.

Who is named. A person is named on an event only when a source documents their role, and that role is shown with them. Trades by unknown traders stay marked Trader unidentified and are never pinned on anyone; a person listed on such an event is there for a different, documented role. Denials, responses and outcomes, including acquittals, dismissals and findings of no wrongdoing, are recorded with each event.

What gets added up. Only individual, dated amounts are added to a total (filled dots). The same rule for every president: specific payments are added (book, speaking and licensing deals, payments from foreign governments), while salaries, pensions and whole-year income totals from tax returns or annual disclosures are shown but not added. Tallies of many events, company valuations, project values and market-wide moves are shown for context but never added (hollow dots). Different kinds of money (trade size, profits, payments, investments, contracts) are never added together. A contract counts as linked to the family or a donor only when the tie existed before the award and was more than a token holding.

Amounts for a period. Some amounts cover a period rather than a day, like a year’s income from a tax return or a token sale that stayed open for weeks. On the timeline they get a bar along the dates they cover. In the running totals they are spread evenly across the period and drawn dashed, because the exact dates aren’t known.

Comparing presidents. Terms differ in length. Insider-trading cases that are charged publish profits; anonymous trades usually don’t, so reported profits are a floor. Research also went deeper in some areas than others; the table below lists what each search did not reach. Something not searched is a gap, not proof that nothing happened.

Checking any number. Each event’s record lists every source and how to check it yourself: exchange data, court dockets, disclosure filings, contract records. On Claude, the Sources panel shows where each number on the page comes from, with its rows to download and each calculation’s formula.

Colors. Each president has a color: red for Trump (a brighter red for his second term, a darker red for his first) and blue for Biden and Obama (a lighter blue for Biden, navy for Obama). On each president’s page that color marks money to the family and connected firms; trades placed just before government news, mostly by others, are gray on every page, and light gray marks background that is never added up. On the Senate and House pages, red is Republican, blue is Democrat and gray is independent. In the bars of trade values, the darker part of a bar is the lowest the total could be and the lighter part runs on to the highest, because members report each trade as a range. Color is never the only cue: every chart also has labels, a key or a list.
